# Where this engine sits — measured, and honestly bounded

Two different questions hide behind "how do we compare":

1. **Against pdf.js / MuPDF.js as a browser viewer** — the capability parity
   table in `README.md` (§ *How it compares*), generated by
   `node scripts/competitive-parity.mjs`. That is a capability count, not a
   speed or fidelity measurement.
2. **Against the native rasterizers** (Ghostscript, MuPDF, Pdfium, Xpdf, the
   Java engines) — the only serious public study is Alfresco's 2018
   engine comparison, and this document is about what can and cannot be
   concluded from it for us.

## The reference study, and its limits

Alfresco (now Hyland) needed to replace Ghostscript in Alfresco Content
Services because of AGPL, and published a comparison of eight engines:
Ghostscript 9.21, MuPDF 1.10a, Xpdf 3.04, Pdfium (2017-04-10), Aspose 17.2,
ICEPdf 6.2, Sejda 3.0.13, PDFBox 2.0.5. Method: 3071 documents / 17,226 pages
from an internal repository, rendered to PNG at 100 dpi, per-engine feature
scores 0–5 against Acrobat Reader by visual comparison, plus a blend-mode error
count and a total rendition time. Conclusion: MuPDF fastest and highest
fidelity, Pdfium second, a clear gap between native and Java engines; they
picked Pdfium for the BSD licence and Google's security response.

Why its ordering cannot be transferred to us:

- **Different job.** Their engines run server-side over a corpus, rasterizing
  to files. Ours runs in the browser tab with no server, no upload, and a
  wasm module that has to fit in a few MB. A wasm build pays a constant factor
  over native for the same integer/float raster work — we have not measured
  that factor, so we do not quote one.
- **Different corpus.** 3071 documents from one repository is a real corpus;
  seven committed fixtures are not. We can report ms/page on a fixture, not a
  corpus mean.
- **Different reference.** Their fidelity score is a human 0–5 against Acrobat.
  Ours is a pixel gate against MuPDF 1.28 (`scripts/compare_render.py`: text
  pages ≤ 1/255 mean absolute channel difference, scans ≤ 3/255, ink within
  2 %). Ours is stricter and reproducible, but it is measured against a
  different oracle, so the numbers are not comparable line for line.
- **Age.** MuPDF 1.10 is from 2016; the current release is 1.28. Anything
  "vs MuPDF" today has to be measured against 1.28, which is what our gate does.

## Their feature rubric, applied to us — with our evidence

Scores in the middle columns are from that study's tables (their maxima: 5, or 6
with the bonus they awarded in two places). "Ours" is what our own test suite
shows; the evidence column is what you can run.

| Their axis | Ghostscript | MuPDF | Pdfium | Ours | Evidence here |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Type1 fonts | 4 | 5 | 5 | **1–2** | PFB is not supported (`ROADMAP` M7); a substitute face is drawn, and `document.warnings` says so |
| TrueType | 4 | 5 | 5 | **4–5** | `ttf`/`cff` embedded: `reader-sample.pdf`, `cjk-cmaps.pdf` inside the 1/255 gate |
| Type1 CID | yes | yes | yes | **yes** | `cfffont.cpp` Type2 interpreter; `type3.pdf`/CID fixtures |
| TrueType CID | yes | yes | yes | **yes** | CID-TT without cmap synthesised from glyph order |
| Type3 | 3 | 5 | 6 | **5** | `type3.pdf` matches MuPDF exactly (ink within 0.001) |
| Flate / LZW / DCT / CCITT / JBIG2 | yes | yes | yes | **yes** | unit checks per filter, `jb2globals.pdf` at ~2/255 |
| JPX (JPEG 2000) | yes | yes | yes | **yes** | vendored OpenJPEG: bit-exact against `opj_decompress` on RGB/grey/CMYK and a tiled, layered codestream; a book cover page lands at 1.06/255 against MuPDF |
| Anti-aliasing | no | yes | yes | **yes** | scanline AA in `pdfraster.cpp`; the 1/255 gate is only reachable with it |
| Blend modes / transparency groups | 3.33 | 3.33 | 2 | **3.3** | all 16 modes; `transparency.pdf` Multiply 0.42/255, `/ca 0.5` group 0.54/255 |
| Shadings (1–7) | — | full | full | **2,3,4,5 only** | types 1/6/7 warn; 4/5 fill but unverified (M4) |
| ICC colour management | partial | full | full | **images only** | `iccp.cpp` wired into images; fills use the CMYK table calibrated against MuPDF (M1) |

Read across: on text, transparency and JBIG2 we are in their top group. On
JPEG 2000, Type1 PFB, shadings and ICC-for-fills we are at the bottom of that
table, and those four cells are what a fidelity review would find first.

## Our own throughput, reproducible

`node scripts/bench.mjs` (100 dpi, the study's resolution, Node 26, one thread,
no worker, no canvas):

```
fixture              pages   bitmap        ms/page   first page (incl. open)   what it is
reader-sample.pdf        3   850x1100         32.6        132 ms              text + vector + image
patterns.pdf             2   417x417          73.3        138 ms              tiling patterns
mesh.pdf                 2   417x417            32         60 ms              mesh shadings (unverified)
jb2globals.pdf           1   865x1155        553.2       1027 ms              JBIG2 scan
type3.pdf                1   417x417             1          1 ms              Type3 glyph procedures
cjk-cmaps.pdf            3   850x1100          5.2         77 ms              CID fonts through CMaps
annot-quads.pdf          4   850x1100          3.7          8 ms              markup annotations
```

The number to watch is **the JBIG2 scan: ~0.55 s per page**. That decoder is
PDFium's, translated to C with `wasm2c` and compiled into the module, and it is
the slowest path in the engine by two orders of magnitude. A repository of
scans is the workload where our throughput would embarrass us, not text.

Everything else lands in tens of milliseconds per page for a 850×1100 bitmap at
100 dpi, which is the range where a viewer needs a page in under a frame or two.

## The honest one-paragraph position

We are not the fastest or the highest-fidelity rasterizer in that study, and we
have no grounds to claim a placement in it. What the evidence supports: on text,
fonts, transparency and JBIG2 our fidelity is at the top of their scale; on
Type1 PFB, mesh/function shadings and ICC-for-fills it is at the
bottom; throughput is a plausible viewer speed for text-heavy pages and bad for
scans. The differentiator is not fidelity — it is that this is the only engine in
that list that runs **inside the browser tab with no server, no upload, MIT
licence and a 3.3 MB module**, which is the constraint that removed Ghostscript
for Alfresco and removes MuPDF for most commercial hosts (AGPL).

## Before you claim anything to a customer

Run the pilot, not the blog post: ~30 real documents from the target deployment,
rendered by both engines, scored on (a) fidelity vs the reference renderer at the
same resolution, (b) time to first page and ms/page, (c) peak heap, (d) failures
and warnings per document, (e) text extraction and accessibility. Write the kill
criterion first — it is what stops a pilot from becoming a migration.
